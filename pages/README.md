
Alan, what is Physics?

Simple question, Silas. In answering it the way I have, I have kept ine important goal in mind: not making you regret asking me!

I think I remember saying that physics is the study of matter and energy, and the relationship between them. That's what the books all say, and they are correct to say it that way. But, what an oversimplification! If we look at life from a certain point of view, physics underlies everything we will ever see around us, so it is hard to imagine asking a bigger question.

We will find that an understanding of physics only takes us so far in grasping the world around us. Cats need to be petted and they have us to pet them... there is no physics equation that explains that, at least I hope there isn't, it would have way too many terms. Life is more than a set of equations and solemn pronouncements of definitions. 

We must also remember that physics is our best model of reality, it is a field of study, and not the thing we are studying. Science changes as we learn more things about the real world, and it's not the world's fault that in a given year or century our knowledge is incomplete. 

It is always going to be that way, too. A million years from now some physicist will have a new idea that explains reality more simply or more compellingly than was thought of before, and then their next thought will be "Poor physicists, they were wrong for a million years, before I came along!"

We ourselves are way further along in our understanding than Aristotle was 2000 years ago. We are even further along than the great Isaac Newton was, 400 years ago. So good for us. But let's not be like that physicist a million years from now and think too much of ourselves just because we know more than Johannes Kepler did in 1593. 

Even if we knew the final laws of physics (and there actually might not be any, it's an open question), that would not explain everything except in a boring way.
