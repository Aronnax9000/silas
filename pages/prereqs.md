# Prerequisites 

I'm going to try to make this book readable for you as a ten year old entering the fifth grade (congratulations on both). 

I will try not to assume too much but here is what I figure you already have been exposed to by now, and a few notes about how the notation we use here, symbols for basic arithmetic operations and so forth, might differ from the math books you might have seen in school, which of course I have not seen.

# Numbers

I assume you know about whole numbers (1, 2, 3 ... ) and fractions like one half (1/2) and one third (1/3).

I assume you know about negative numbers, numbers with a minus sign in front of them. 

Since you love pi, I am also assuming you know about the "real" numbers, that include not just whole numbers and fractions, but irrational numbers like pi, which cannot be expressed as fractions.

I am assuming you have been exposed to the "real number line", a horizontal line running left to right, with zero in the middle and negative numbers to the left of zero, and positive numbers to the right of zero.
